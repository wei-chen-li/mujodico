Scripted witnesses for the hard-task benchmark

Each task has a hand-scripted, open-loop control sequence that reaches the goal under the solver's true contact law (MJX Hunt-Crossley SAP, smoothing 0). These are those rollouts for seed 0, replayed in real time. They exist only to show the tasks are physically solvable; no solver output appears here. Error is the success metric at the final step (success < 0.01).

Left panel: visual geometry, the way the scene looks.
Right panel: collision geometry only, from a second camera angle. This is what the contact law sees. Hidden helper spheres are shown in yellow.
Red dots: contact points (MuJoCo's collision detection on the same states).
Green ghost: the goal pose of whatever the cost scores.

GripperCubeStack

4.5 s · error 1.8e-6 · start 5.3

Cube stacking

Grasp red cube A (5 cm, 100 g), lift, rotate the wrist, set A on blue B turned 90°, open. Watch the pads stop at the cube faces and A sit flat on B.

Check
  • Pads do not collide with the table or with cube B. A wrist-height floor keeps upright pads above the table instead.
  • Cubes touch the table and each other through 4 bottom corner spheres only.

GripperPegInsert

4.5 s · error 2.0e-4 · start 7.2

Peg insertion

A 3×3×12 cm, 60 g peg lies on its side. Grasp near one end, roll the wrist 90°, insert 4.5 cm. Clearance is 3 mm at the face centres and about 4.5 mm on the diagonals.

Check
  • The hole walls are two rings of 4 spheres; the block between them has no collision.
  • The lying peg rests on two end spheres, so on the table it rolls like a cylinder.
  • The peg can spin about ±23° in the hole.

GripperDrawer

6.0 s · error 1.7e-3 · start 12.4

Drawer insertion

A 7×6×4 cm drawer lies on its side. Grasp it by the end faces, turn it upright, set it in front of the slot, then push it in with both pads. Clearance is 4 mm per side.

Check
  • The pads pass through the cabinet walls (no pad-cabinet contact), so a solver could carry the drawer straight in.
  • The drawer is dropped from about 1.5 cm, because horizontal pads cannot go below the wrist floor.
  • The cabinet is 6 spheres (mouth, back, back stop, ceiling).

GripperLegTwist

6.0 s · error 1.5e-3 · start 11.0

Table-leg twisting

Pick up the 14 cm leg, insert its round stud into the socket, then twist by circling the wrist around the leg axis. A key pin on the leg drives a nut (hinge) between two dog pins to +90°.

Check
  • There is no thread advance; the nut turns but does not descend. It holds its angle by viscous damping only.
  • The key pin passes through the socket's ring spheres (the gaps act as the keyway).

Tool use with an Allegro hand

SimToolReal (RSS 2026): iiwa + Sharpa hand, here an Allegro on a 6-DoF wrist, dt 0.04 s

AllegroHammer

6.0 s · error 2.6e-9 · start 0.16

Hammering a nail

Pinch the handle near its balance point, lift, correct for the hammer's roll in the grasp, then press the head on the nail to drive it 2 cm. The nail is a slide joint with 4 N of dry friction.

Check
  • The script presses rather than swings. At dt 0.04 s an impact is spread over a full step, so a swing transfers little impulse.
  • The nail block is visual only. The nail goes 2 cm below the block's top face, so in the left panel the head ends up inside the block.
  • The handle rests propped up by a knob, so no in-hand rotation is needed. The fingertips can pass through the head.

AllegroScrewdriver

6.0 s · error 9.5e-4 · start 2.47

Turning a screwdriver

Lift, roll the wrist 90° so the blade points down, insert it between two posts on the screw head, twist the screw (a hinge with dry friction) by π/2.

Check
  • The slot drives one way only, and the slot direction was chosen so the twist fits the wrist range without a regrasp.
  • This was the hardest to certify: fast wrist rotations make the tool creep out of the pinch, so the script rotates slowly with drift corrections.
  • The blade can sink below the posts (no blade-head contact).

AllegroSpatulaFlip

6.0 s · error 1.7e-3 · start 1.79

Spatula toast flip

Lay the blade flat behind the toast, slide under its crust edge, tilt up while keeping the blade under it, push on until the toast falls over onto its top face.

Check
  • The toast collides only through 4 spheres. The blade can touch only one raised crust sphere and passes through the toast's underside elsewhere.
  • The goal assumes an ideal roll-over (8.9 cm along +x, upside down).

AllegroPenBoard

6.0 s · error 1.6e-5 · start 0.22

Reorient a pen and write a letter

The solver only accepts a fixed final goal, so the ink is physical: 5 push-pins in an "L" that stay down once pressed. Roll the pen upright in the pinch, then dab each pin flush.

Check
  • The pen is a thick marker (radius 16 mm) with inflated rotational inertia; a 12 mm pen squirted out of the pinch.
  • Pins are gravity-compensated and damped, with no spring. The pen tip passes through the table.

AllegroBrushSweep

5.2 s · error 2.0e-3 · start 1.50

Sweep debris into a dustpan

The brush starts with its bristles sideways. Roll it bristles-down (about 60° at the wrist, about 30° inside the pinch), then sweep two crumbs against the pan's back wall.

Check
  • The crumbs slide on x/y joints at a fixed height with damping (no rolling, no table contact).
  • The pan is only its back wall, and the brush passes through it. The brush is light (28 g).

AllegroEraserWipe

4.8 s · error 1.1e-5 · start 0.18

Erase a whiteboard

The ink is a 2×3 grid of raised pins. Roll the eraser about 90° so the felt faces down, then press the pins flush in two placements (the felt covers one column at a time).

Check
  • A handle bar was added to the eraser; a bare box cannot be pinched palm-down and rolled 90°.
  • The middle finger passes through the eraser (left out to stay within the contact budget). The felt passes through the table.
Applies to every task
  • The hand or gripper is filtered out of contact with the target (nail, screw, toast, crumbs, pins), so only the tool can move it.
  • Fingertips have spheres that collide with the table; the other finger links, the palm and the tools are not checked against fixtures.
  • Each task is limited to 16 contacts (solver GPU memory grows with contact count), so contacts that rarely matter were replaced by joint ranges or dropped. Each task's docstring in docs/handoff_2026-11-23_bench/scripts lists them.
  • Grasped tools roll slowly inside the pinch under the true law; the scripts correct for it with a grasp offset measured once.
